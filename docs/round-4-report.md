### 2. Noon from the Short's camera (half)

What I tried and what the geometry refuses, in the order of the request:

- **A visible disc.** The sun at noon is at 24° over the south. The camera looks 15° north of west with a field of 23° by 40°: **the sun is 105.6° (P0), 104.0° (P1) and 103.4° (P3) off its axis**. No pose that keeps the wall in the frame has the disc in it from the east; a take with the disc has the camera on the north side of the wall (the sun over the head of the glazing) or a first shot that pans from the sun to the wall. The film's decision; the sun's disc, shafts and glint are in the lab for any camera that looks south.
- **A glint on the glass.** The camera sees the glass at 4° to 6° from its plane; the reflection of that view looks west-south-west at the horizon, not at the sun. None.
- **The shadow of the frame and mullions on the cut.** The cut's plane (x = 0) is edge-on to the sun (the sun is due south; the cut's normal is east): the angle of incidence is 90°, there is no light and no shadow to draw, and a drawn one would be a lie on the false colour.
- **On the room floor.** The room floor is in the wall's own shadow (the sun is south, the room is north of 60 cm of concrete): a render with the key light alone shows it black. That is the story of the film (the sun never reaches the room; the heat will), and it is why the room is lit by the sky only.

What the frame does see, and what I did with it: the ground behind the room, which the sun lights. **29 boulders of the plateau's granite with the sun's planar shadow** (the `site`'s boulder builder, asked for smooth normals) stand where the right of the frame looks (four of them on the line of the camera, at 10, 16, 28 and 40 m); the shadows follow the state's sun, fade with it, are none at night and none under a cover; a deeper clear sky laid by the camera over a haze band; the black face. `docs/qa/p4-01`, `p4-09`. ### 5. The coda's sky

The finding: the sky's own ramp (0.03 a frame) was not what lagged the picture at ×11 150. The beam's warmth ramp (0.03 over 22°) and the beam's (0.04) bound the afternoon long before the sky's ramp was steep, so the picture was half an hour of sun behind at 14:00 and two hours at 17:00. Relaxed (the beam 0.06, the warmth 0.12: a few levels of the picture) with the sky's 0.032 (the most the lab's own bound of 12 levels a channel on the horizon row allows: 0.036 gives 13) and the lamps' 0.065 kept (0.09 turned the key light's direction from the sun's to the moon's in a few frames: 1.1 % of the coda's pixels over 12 levels in a frame, 0.04 % at 0.065).

Numbers (Node, the real sun, the coda's ×11 150 from noon): the sky half way to its night after the sun's by **93 → 37 min**, in its night by **154 → 93 min** (18:36 against the sun's 17:03); in the page (the Short's P3 camera, 540 × 960) the mean luma at 17:00 142 → 111, at 18:00 125 → 90, the picture's half-way at 18:31 → 17:23, 90 % down at 19:39 → 18:37; the largest one-frame mean step 3.7 → 3.2; **the worst frame over 12 levels 0.024 → 0.041 %** (the film's bar is its dissolve's 15 %). `docs/qa/p4-05` has the dusk frame by frame, with the warm band at the horizon.

**What the law cannot give.** At 17:00 the sky is still a dusk (the day factor 0.44; 0.16 at 18:00) where the film holds 17:00 as night at 30 s. The sun crosses the sky's twenty degrees in 22 frames; the sky may not move by more than 0.032 of its range a frame, so its dusk takes 31 frames and ends 9 frames (56 minutes of the clock) after the sun's. Reaching the clock needs a quicker dusk (0.06 a frame: about 22 levels on the horizon's red, against the 12 the tests hold) or a picture that looks ahead of the clock (the shown sun at the state's plus the pace times a third of a second), which would break « exactly the state's at ×3 600 and under » and « a pace change moves nothing ». The lag is a function of the pace, which the film chooses: ×3 600 and ×5 000 none, ×6 000 4 / 10 minutes, **×8 000 14 / 40 minutes (day factor 0.26 at 17:00)**, ×11 150 37 / 93. A coda at ×8 000 would have the sky within 40 minutes of its clock everywhere; that is for the video engineer and the owner.
