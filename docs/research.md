>
> **Tags.** _reported_ = said by a source. _derived_ = computed from reported figures, formula given. _assumption_ = a choice we make. _estimate_ = no source, reasoning given. _model_ = the output of someone's simulation, never a measurement. _by eye_ = read from a printed curve, ± a few percent. **Model column**: `exact` = arithmetic, a unit test retrieves it; `compare` = the model reports its own value against this one, never tuned to it; `input` = it goes into the model as a parameter; `find` = to be found by the model, no target; `—` = context only.

## 0. Summary and the plan against the bar

### 0.1 The house

**The 1967 CNRS prototype at Odeillo (Font-Romeu-Odeillo-Via, Pyrénées-Orientales, 1,550 m): one storey, four main rooms, 76 m², 300 m³, with 48 m² of south-facing collector: a 60 cm concrete wall painted black, behind two sheets of glass 12 cm in front of it, with a duct at the floor and a duct near the ceiling.** Built by Félix Trombe's solar laboratory with the architect Jacques Michel. Heated in part by the sun and for the rest by electricity, with thermostats at 20 °C. It is the first well-known house with what became known as the Trombe wall (S20), and its builders measured it hour by hour in 1974–75.

Why this one (§8, Q1): it is the only house I found for which I could read, together, **the inventors' own table of dimensions, a day-by-day energy balance, four multi-day recordings of temperature and flow, and a monthly efficiency curve for a full year**, in one public, free, primary document (S1, a United States government report on OSTI). It is also a machine you can cut open, which a well-insulated box is not. The second candidate, the first Passive House in Darmstadt, keeps a place as the comparator (§1.3, K62–K70).

**What this replaces.** The roadmap (§5.6) asks for a generic house section with four devices: Trombe wall, earth tube, solar dryer, solar cooker. The lead asks for one real house. No real house I found has all four. I keep the wall as the machine, the earth tube as a clearly marked what-if, and propose that the dryer and the cooker leave this lab (Q2).

### 0.2 What the check of the sources changed

1. **There is a primary source, and it is better than anything derived from it.** Everything popular says « about 70 % » and « two feet of concrete ». S1 gives ρ, λ, c, the glass, the gap, the element, the ducts, the rooms and the volume, and it was hiding in a 1976 conference volume on OSTI. **Official dimensions exist before modelling, from the inventors themselves.**
2. **The textbook formula retrieves the inventors' measurements, with their own material data.** With ρ = 2,200, λ = 1.75, c = 921 (K11), a daily wave moves at 4.03 cm/h and arrives through 0.60 m after 14.9 h, through 0.37 m after 9.2 h. S1 measured 3.7–4 cm/h, 14–16 h and 9–10 h (K23, K24, K71). This is the lab's backbone.
3. **The inventors call their own wall wrong.** « The time of transmission of the 1967 wall (0.60 m, 14–16 hours), is too long. The one of the 1974 wall (0.37 m, 9–10 hours), is too short » (S1 p. 204). They ask for 40 to 45 cm. A wall thickness slider with these marks is the first thing a visitor should touch.
4. **On Christmas Eve 1974 the wall gave back 478 % of the sun it received that day** (K31, read on the page image). The authors call that value « aberrant »: it is yesterday's sun. It is the best fact I found, and the lesson that follows is real: a wall with a memory has no daily efficiency (S1 says balances need 24 h at least, and printed recordings show why).
5. **« 70 % » is one of at least five numbers.** The inventors: 60–70 % of the heating load at Odeillo, « depending on the year », 35–45 % at Chauvency-le-Château (K22). A colleague at the same conference: 75–90 %, « estimated » (K41). Wikipedia: 70 %, through a 2013 book (K43). A 1978 simulation paper: « of the order of 40 per cent » in winter (K42, seen in a search summary only). **My hand balance (K76–K78): the wall alone covers about half the load on a run of clear winter days and about a third of a whole season; the rest needs the south windows and the occupants, whose areas and amounts nobody publishes.** I do not settle it. This is the question the model answers, and the honest answer to the film's question.
6. **The roadmap's « aha » is true, for a different reason from the usual one.** « The low sun heats, the high sun is stopped » suggests an overhang. The one photograph I have shows no deep overhang. At Odeillo the geometry does the job: the noon sun meets the wall at 24° from its normal in December and 71° in June (K34, derived), the glass reflects more of it, and **PVGIS gives this wall 1.6 times more sun in January than in June** (K56). S1's efficiency falls from about 36 % in winter to about 15 % in June (K39), and the authors say plainly that this suits passive heating. **In December the wall gets 2.2 times what a flat roof gets.**
7. **Morse's 1881 patent is not a Trombe wall.** Popular pages say Morse patented « the concept ». Morse has the black absorber, the glass and the two openings (S9), and **a thin plate, no store**. The heavy wall is what the 1967 house adds. The patent's author was a zoologist (S10). Good story, one precise sentence.
8. **The dates conflict, and a primary source settles most of them.** The Coyote video says « en 1956 » (V1, 1:30) and **a wall of 20 cm** (V1, 2:00, 2:30), against S1's 1967, 60 cm and 37 cm. Its 1956 probably comes from Wikipedia FR's sentence that Morse's patent lay ignored until then: my guess. 1962 is a « cold house » experiment (s). The first CNRS houses are of 1966–67 (S1, S5). The patent's priority is 29 June 1971 (S8). **The protected monument is the 1974 building**, not the 1967 house (S4). Do not mix them.
9. **The search tools manufacture false figures.** A Passive House Institute document titled « Passive House Darmstadt Kranichstein » (database document 583) is the form for **Brook House in Exeter, 2024**, with the wrong title. A search summary handed me its U-values, 77 % and n50 0.52 h⁻¹ as Kranichstein's. I read the PDF; they are not. This is the kind of thing the gravity QA found (a figure from the wrong row): check the document, not the summary.
10. **The 1967 houses may still stand, and IGN's open data gives their footprint.** Near the laboratory, BD TOPO lists two identical one-storey dwellings, **15.0 × 6.6 m, long side due east–west, date of appearance 1968**, and the aerial view shows twin pitched roofs. The interior this leaves (about 80 m² before partitions) and S1's 76 m² agree. **No document I read ties them to the prototypes.** It is my reading, flagged, and I use their dimensions as the model's outline with that flag (§3, Q3). These may be private homes: their identifiers and position stay in my private cache, this public file says no more than this, the `plateau` is composed rather than mapped, and neither the lab nor a film points at them.
11. **`sim/thermal` covers the wall and the loop, not the sun, and not the altitude.** `stackDraft` calls `airRho` at sea-level pressure: at 1,550 m that overstates the draft by about a fifth (§5.1).
12. **The earth tube has little to do at this altitude, by the numbers.** Mean air 6.6 °C, summer months 13–16 °C (K55): a pipe gives air near the soil's mean, about 7 °C (estimate). It would temper winter air and has little summer heat to fight. An architect in the roadmap's own video has never fitted one and would spend the money elsewhere (V3, K88–K91). The model will say; it is an honest thing to show.

### 0.5 The two guides

The modes stay, the frame is locked; both are filled with what can be said.

- **Assembly (20): how it was built.** No source gives the 1967 building sequence. **Our reading**, from S1's materials, Photo 1 and usual practice, with the few facts that are reported: gravel concrete, black acrylic, steel frame, glazing at 12 cm; and S1's warning that concrete poured in place stays wet for years, which cost the 1974 wall about a tenth of its yield.
- **Installation (10): how a wall joins a site and a year.** Facing south, the winter horizon, collector area per m³, insulation first, winter and summer positions, first winters of drying, backup, fresh air, cleaning, how to measure it. Reported where S1, S2 and S5 speak, _usual practice_ where they don't.
- **Honest, and said once at the top of each guide:** this is a reading for learning, not a procedure. Every step ends on its origin (_reported_, _usual practice_, _our reading_), as in the sand and solar labs, a data test can check it. No rebar, no mix design, no torque, no footing size. A 1.3 t/m² wall is a structural matter for an engineer.
- **The mechanics still hold:** nothing floats at any step (the glass needs its frame, the ducts need their box in the formwork), and each id of `house`, `wall` and `room` is installed in exactly one step, except what-if parts.

**Lessons:** 1 `the-black-wall` (what the sun does behind glass, the two ways out: through the wall and up the gap, Morse's plate against Trombe's store) · 2 `the-slow-wave` (4 cm an hour, lag and damping, too late and too early, why 40–45 cm, why the concrete must be dry) · 3 `christmas-eve` (**478 %**: what an efficiency means for something with a memory, why S1 needs days) · 4 `low-sun-high-sun` (**the « aha »**: more sun on the wall in January than in June, the glass that refuses, 36 % and 15 %, the summer position) · 5 `can-a-house-heat-itself` (**the film's question**: who says 40, 60–70, 75–90; what S1's 1.63 → 1.00 W/m³·K buys; wall, windows, people, electricity; the same answer at Chauvency; the Darmstadt house's 9 kWh/m² by another road) · 6 `the-night` (reverse thermosiphon, Kelbaugh's first winter, flaps; LASL's test rooms with and without vents: a 26 °F swing peaking at 4 p.m. against 9 °F at 10 p.m.; the loss through glass at night) · 7 `fresh-air-and-the-ground` (the loop recirculates the room's air, it brings none; the earth tube, what it can and cannot do at 1,550 m; condensation and radon). Two quiz questions each.

**Spoken guide:** 56 parts × about 270 characters, four star parts (`storage-wall`, `air-gap`, `upper-duct`, `inner-wall-face`) at about 400, four worlds, an introduction: about 22,000 characters per language. Character to follow: **one wave of warmth** that leaves the black face at noon and walks through the concrete, 4 cm an hour, and reaches a sleeping house at three in the morning. Anonymous as usual. Said once: reconstruction, model figures, one warning about structure, one at fresh air. Written after the checkpoint, from the final sheets. Local voice (`ROADMAP` §10).

### 5.2 The model, in short

Nodes: outer glass, inner glass, gap air (one or several along its height), wall grid, room air, room mass (estimate), outside, sky, ground. Absorbed sun on the plate: `I(t) · τ(θ) · α`. Plate to gap: convection and radiation to the inner glass. Gap: flow from draft against losses, `Q = ṁ cₚ (Tout − Tin)`. Inner face: S1's films or the kit's, and radiation with ε = 0.94. Room: `C dT/dt = Qt + Qr + Qc + Q_windows + Q_gains + Q_aux − G·V (T − Tout)`, `Q_aux` holding 20 °C. **`G·V` is the whole-house figure, and it already holds the wall's own loss in some way nobody defines**: to avoid counting the wall twice, the model states what it does, and tests both.

**Report four lines separately, always: wall by air, wall by conduction, windows, people; then electricity.** That is how K22, K42, K76–K78 get settled.

## 7. Safety, law and credits

**No wording here or in the lab promises safety. The lab teaches how the thing works and names what to ask; it is not a plan, and nothing in it says a wall like this is safe to build or to live behind.**

**Hazards, for the sheets and one warning each, said once where the gesture is risky.**

- **The weight.** 1.32 t per m² of wall, 63 t on one side of a light house, on a slope (K72). Foundations and seismic design are for a structural engineer. Said once, at `storage-wall`, and at the top of the guides. **BRIEF §8's spirit: no footing, rebar or mix given.**
- **Fresh air and carbon monoxide.** **The thermocirculation loop brings no outdoor air**: S1's « fresh air » (p. 201) means cool air from the room, a trap for translators. A house still needs ventilation, whatever S1 is silent about. A fuel appliance in a tight room pulls its air from somewhere: Kelbaugh's fireplace « combusts warm room air and pulls in cold outside air », and he would add a direct air feed (S2). Said once, at `fresh-air-vent`; a warning on carbon monoxide where any fire appears (it does not in this house, as far as I know; the chimney on the aerial view is unexplained).
- **Heat behind glass.** The outer face reaches about 65 °C with air moving (K37, by eye). With the vents closed: `find`. Paint and sealants age there. Vents closed in summer with no route out: the room is not the place to find out. Said once at `summer-flap`.
- **Overheating.** S1 shows no summer room data. France's RE2020 has a summer indicator (S23). Mass with no route for its heat can make a hot week worse; a fact to show in the model, without a verdict on real houses.
- **Vents and fire, smoke, insects, dust.** A duct through a fire wall or between floors is a matter of the rules of the place. **Not read.** The 1974 building has several floors, and I don't know how it was handled.
- **Glass.** Large panes at height, cleaning the inner sheets (S21: a Sète house was altered to open its upper windows for cleaning). Falls, and safety glass: usual practice, the rules of the place. Said once.
- **Moisture.** Wet concrete behind glass for one or two years (K33). The wall's drying has a thermal cost and may have others: **not read**.
- **Mains.** Electric backup: qualified electrician, the rules of the place. Said once.
- **Earth tube.** Condensation and mould where a pipe has a low point without a drain, filter upkeep, microbial load low but not nil according to one thesis, and **radon**: Font-Romeu is listed in zone 3 of the French map by a secondary page (S24, s). Tubes in rock on a granite plateau deserve the question: an airtight pipe and a measurement. Said once, in lesson 7. V3's reservations appear once, credited, once a clean transcript confirms them.
- **Scald, gas, pressure:** none in this house.

**The people.** The 1974 building is **private property, lived in** (S4). The houses I take for the prototypes may be homes. The lab says « near the laboratory at Odeillo »; it gives no address beyond what Mérimée publishes for the monument, no aerial view, no invitation to go and look. **No figure from the 1974 owners' lives; S4's names appear only as S4 gives them.**
