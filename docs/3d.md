# The 3D worlds

Four worlds built from `src/three/dims.ts` (metres, the one source; every constant tagged `reported`, `derived`, `estimate` or `assumption`, and what the simulation owns is imported from `src/sim/machine.ts`, held by `dims.test.ts`). `src/three/index.ts` exports `createSiteScene`, `createHouseScene`, `createWallScene`, `createRoomScene`, all `lazyModule`s, and `WORLD` (the `SceneDef` fields of each). **56 parts**, the ids frozen in `docs/research.md` §4 and held in `src/three/ids.ts` (`PART_IDS`): `site` 11, `house` 18 (three of them the earth tube's what-if), `wall` 14, `room` 13. No text in any texture, no canvas, no file: the kit's labels are the only words. No dimension is drawn in any world.

**The setting's id is `site`, not `plateau`.** The kit frames a world called `site` by its authored home pose, gives it its camera planes and a camera that stays above the ground (`docs/kit-requests.md`, 7). Its label is « Le plateau » / « The plateau », its parts keep the research's ids (`PLATEAU_PARTS`). The data role writes `scene: 'site'` for those eleven.

## Frames

**House frame:** x east, y up from the **top of the floor slab**, z south, the origin on the plan's centre; the south front is the plane z = +3.3, the north wall's outer face z = −3.3. **Element frame:** x across the bay from its centre, y as in the house, z = 0 on the outer face of the steel frame and negative into the house. `site` and `house` and `room` draw the house in its own frame with no offset (the twin is moved 38 m along x); `wall` draws the element in its own.

The front is twelve bays of 1.27 m (15.24 m, against the open data's 15.0 ± 3): nine collector bays, one window, two of recessed entrance (`BAY_KINDS`). Nine bays of net glass (the frame's bars taken out) come to 47.1 m², against the reported 48 m² (within 2 %; `dims.test.ts`). The ground falls 2.0 m over the 6.6 m of the footprint (`dims.ts`: −0.3 m at the north wall, −2.3 m at the south front, both under the floor), the low end of the open data's 2 to 3.5 m; the foundation steps with it. _Settled at integration: `dims.ts` had the ground at −2.0 m at the south front, a fall of 1.7 m, below the open data's range; the south end is now `GROUND_NORTH − 2.0`, and `dims.test.ts` holds the fall at 2.0 m and the eaves, from the south ground, inside the open data's 5.8 to 6.7 m._

The element's meshes are **instanced**: one mesh per slot, one instance per bay, so the nine bays cost the draw calls of one. When the visitor moves the wall's thickness, the gap, the sheets or the ducts, `relayout` makes each slot's geometry again from the spec (`elementSpecOf` of the state and the parameters): the glass stays where it is, the inner face moves into the room, the foundation, the head band, the window's reveals and the recess follow. The sample bay (the seventh, in the living room) carries the instruments; the others do not.

`machine.test.ts` holds the shared pieces within 3 mm: **every mesh of the 14 parts of the element in `wall` against `house`, `room` and `site`; every exterior group of the house across the three worlds; the twin against the prototype moved by 38 m**.

**Attached and resting** (`machine.test.ts`, measured on the meshes of the body): the foundation's top is the frame's foot; the head band's top is where the rafters stand, whose tails follow the roof's underside; the chimney stands on the ceiling's insulation and its top is `CHIMNEY.above` = 0.9 m over the ridge line (measured from `Y_RIDGE`, as `dims.ts` now says; the drawing's elevation reads it the same way); the north wall stands on the ground; the gables' steps follow the ground within 5 cm; the balcony's stair has `STAIR_STEPS` = 14 treads (`dims.ts`, the one number the 3D, the sheet PL-H01 and the part sheet read; held against the ground by a test: the last tread stands 9 cm over the land, one more would go under it), stringers under it; the downpipes end at the ground; each thing of the yard stands on the land at its own foot; every tree and every heliostat stands on the land at its own place (5 mm); no heliostat's panel goes into the ground at any sun; the furnace and the 1974 building stand on flat shelves; the junction box carries the thermocouples' bundle and the anemometer's cable; the beads lie inside the concrete in the section's plane and the leads run to the room face; the air probes hang in the middle of the gap from the head beam; the anemometer's tip is in the middle of the **lower** duct's tunnel (the paper's « centre of the lower duct », S1 p. 203; it was drawn in the upper one) and its cable runs up the wall's face to the junction box's underside; the flap's cord reaches its crank and its eye, open or shut; the flap closes the pocket in winter and the duct in summer; the probe's cable, the recorder and the thermostat's wire end where they say; the jet starts at the upper grille and the return ends at the lower; the furniture stands on the floor.

## What is not a part follows a part

The kit hides, ghosts and explodes parts, never the props round them. `follow.ts` makes a prop follow the part that carries it, each frame, from the world's `update`: hidden or wire (a step not yet built in a guide) hides it, an explode moves it with the carrier, a ghost leaves it in place. `house`: the furniture, the other rooms' beds and kitchen, the recorder, the armchair and the floor's finish follow `floor-slab`; the heater, the panel, the thermostat, the vent, a picture and the skirting follow `north-wall`; the coat and a picture on the cross walls follow `interior-partitions`; the curtains and the wall's face plate, the streams, the probes follow `collector-element`; the ceiling and its lamp follow `roof-insulation`; the chair and the pots on the balcony follow `entrance-balcony`. The yard's things (the line, the barrow, the bed) stand on the land, which is not a part: they stay. `room`: the floor's furniture follows `room-floor`; what hangs on a wall goes with the wall (above). `wall`: the coat follows `storage-wall`; the beads and leads of the thermocouples follow their part (they stayed, `visible = true` every frame). The section's cut faces follow the part they cut in the same way (`Caps.add(part, …)` in `house/scene.ts`: the roof's boards, rafters and slates are the roof's, the wall's face the north wall's, the slab's the slab's, the hardcore and footings the foundation's, the head band the south front's; only the earth's own face is the land's), so with the cut on and a part isolated nothing of the section stands in the air. A test per prop (`standing.test.ts`) shows it with its carrier alone and not with another part alone, and the explode moves it with the carrier.

## The section

`section.ts` (from the mass heater lab): a `ClipSet` of planes that never change in number and that « off » moves far away, so pressing the tool compiles nothing; caps are flat rectangles in unclipped materials. `house`: the plane x = `CUT_X` keeps the west, the tool toggles it, the caps are the earth (its strata, laid by depth under the surface so they follow the slope), the foundation, the hardcore, the slab and its tile, the ceiling and its insulation, the north wall, the head band, the roof's boards, rafters and slates, and **the element's own faces**: the sill, the wall and the head carry the model's temperatures, with the paint's line, the frame's bars, the sheets' edges and the sleeves' plates. The beads and the leads of the thermocouples lie in the plane and show only while it is on. `wall`: always on. `room`: its two planes run along the cross walls (the other rooms are cut off at the bay lines) and its ends have faces; the section is not a tool there.

**The pops were not the camera: they were the simulation's sun.** The tool steps the app on a pinned clock at the lab's own speed (×3600: two minutes of the model's time a frame), so a 90-frame move takes the sun through its sunrise or its sunset, and the frame of the pop is the sim's hour, not the camera's (found by running the dolly with the clock at ×1: 1.4, 1.4, 1.3 in the three worlds, no pop; and with the camera still at ×3600: a one-frame jump of 4 to 10 grey levels in every world at sim hour 8.03, against 0.01 to 0.5 round it). Three things jumped: the key light was the sun's above −1° and the moon's under it (direction and strength changed in one frame, twice a day); the 63 heliostats flipped from tracking to face down at 2° in one frame; and the beam's share jumped in one step when the model's ridge let the sun out. Now the beam and the moon are laid in one light (`sun.ts`: each at its own strength, the direction between them), the beam comes in over the sun's first fourteen degrees, its share (`clarity`) is eased at 2.5 a second over the ridge's step, the sky comes up over twenty degrees (the lamps and the stars keep their shorter curve), and the heliostats turn over the sun's first seven degrees. **The orbit's shimmer was partly the same**: with the clock at ×1 the orbit is 2.02 % (`site`), 1.55 % (`house`), 0.57 % (`wall`), 2.68 % (`room`), against the ram pump's 1.0 to 1.6 %.

What shimmers is found by hiding groups one at a time (`room`, orbit, clock ×1: 3.75 % with everything; 0.63 % with the `house` shell hidden; furniture 3.61, ceiling 3.75, floor 3.72, armchair 3.65, recorder 3.73; ambient occlusion, shadows and supersampling off: 3.75, 3.37, 3.75): **it is the shell, the south front's glazing bars and the window frames (22 mm of steel at ten metres is two pixels), not the room's contents**. Not fixed: a bar that thin is sub-pixel at that distance whatever the geometry; the room's overview is closer in the browser than in this measure. The `house` dolly is the same bars. **Weak point for the reviewer.**

Stills (headless Chrome for Testing, the review page, Metal): `~/.cache/lab-review/bioclimatic-house-lab/3d/`, 84 files, `<world>-00-home`, then the world's variants (cut, explode, X-ray, flows, summer, night, ground level, the thick wall, three sheets, the earth tube on), then every part `<world>-pNN-<id>` as the stage frames it. ### Tests added

### Still open

- **The sky is a screen-space gradient**, not tied to the horizon (a texture the renderer paints as one plane); a camera that looks down sees none of it. Kit request 18 (`backdrop`) answers it properly; the `wall`'s horizon is hidden by the fog.
- **The `wall` dolly's spike of 6.6 in the kit's tool** (above): a fade, not a pop, and the tool cannot tell. To keep the tool's number under 2 the sky would have to stand still.
- **The black face at grazing** reads a satin charcoal, lighter than a flat black, under the sun and the shafts; its `roughness` (0.56) and `envMapIntensity` (0.5) are the knobs. **The glass at grazing** reads milky white: it is a mirror at that angle.
- **The shafts are a hint**, `SHAFT_GAIN` 0.3: against the paper's pale ground they read as soft slanted bands at the left of the glass, not as rays; the sun's disc is only in view when the camera looks south (the Short's camera looks west, so the sun is not in its frame).
- **The road of the plateau** wears its gravel three times its tile (`u = x / 3`): left, listed in `uvscale.test.ts`; metre-true it would shimmer more.
- **Not done, in the order the brief gave**: the house's roof (slate has no relief), the snow and the grass, the furniture (the linen, wool, wood, bark, leaf and rug are laid from 0 to 1 on their object: `OWN_UV`). The house and the room got the shared materials only; their overviews are the same to a few tenths of a point. Where the pass stopped: the walls' render got its relief; each further step costs a map and a program per world for a surface seen at a metre in no shot yet.
- **The first frame's +8 %** is two thirds the maps; the old ones are heavier still (`masonry` 258 ms, `granite` 180, `strata` 148, `rug` 114 at this profile, built in the first world whether it wears them or not): a lazy fill would give the budget back (every one of the 18 maps the first frame builds is worn by the house, so only the other worlds would gain).
- **The real phone**: not measured.

## How a part is framed

Every part has a `FRAMING` in its world's `scene.ts`: a `viewDir`, so that a click or the voice's `[[frame:id]]` reads the part from the same side whatever the camera was doing, and a `frame` where the part's whole box would keep the camera too far (the house, the twin, the furnace, the field, the road, the trees, the ridges). The setting's far parts name a box of their own.

## The camera

- **The opening is the overview.** The kit frames a world once it is built (`Stage.frameAll`, from the module's `home` direction, on the box of every part that is not hidden) and the « Vue d'ensemble » button does the same: so the opening and the button agree, and the whole point is the box. The house's box is the house, its balcony, its stair and the earth tube's window in the ground (it stays inside the house's width, `standing.test.ts`). The home direction is 24° off the south front's normal and 20° up, `homePadding` 0.8: the south front covers 61 % of a 1280 px view and 60 to 65 % of a 390 or 768 px one (it was a third; the opening framed the old tube, which stretched the box, and the button then framed it again, at 56 %). A test holds the share at every size, with the tube off and on. That share is the south front's box, the removed half included: with the section on (how the house opens) the east half of the front is not drawn, so the house one sees is about 40 % of a phone's width, the rest being the cut earth; the kit cannot know that the half is gone (`PartRegistry.bounds` reads the geometry), so the box stays whole.
- **The plateau opens on the setting**: the camera stands south-west and low (`home` of `site/scene.ts`), the houses in front, the 1974 building to the west and the furnace with the front of its field to the east, each with a main label (the furnace's `wide`: on a phone it is in the view and its label waits for « Toutes »). The house is small there (about 40 px wide at 1280: the furnace is 280 m away); its label « La maison → » and the sun's two arcs round it find it. A narrow view backs off along the same line (`maxDistance` 1,500 m).
- **A chosen part is framed to be read**: the world's `partPadding` is 1.6 for the house (it was 2.6: the south front stood at 64 m, a quarter of the picture, and the foundation at 65 m); every house part fills between 38 and 60 % of the view at 1280 and 390 px (`partView` in `framing.ts` replicates the kit's framing; a test holds 30 % at least).
- **The camera turns round the selected part in the plateau**: the ground, the meadow, the road, the far ridges and the far pines are not hit (`raycast` off), so a press on them is a press on nothing and the kit keeps the target. Pressing on another part (a heliostat, a boulder, the other house) still turns the camera round it, as the kit does everywhere.
- **The east gable with the section on**: the east half is taken away and the gable with it, so the part is drawn as a pale ghost with its edges, out of the section's clip (`house/scene.ts`, `setGable`): it can be chosen, outlined and read; with the cut off it is plain render again.

- **The `room` world's camera is inside the room.** At home it stands in the north-west corner looking south-east, so that the wall with the ducts and the recorder is at the centre of the picture, the table and the armchair in front of it; the north wall and the west cross wall are behind it. It is a serpentine of five runs 0.8 m apart, 50 m with its riser, north of the house and west of the section's plane, so that it is there with the cut on and does not stretch the overview. It is **always there**, pale (`setOn(false)`), with its labels « et si… », and firm when the scenario runs: the parts have their size, their box and their framing whatever the scenario says (before, they collapsed to a point inside the house and their chips framed it, and the Specs said 0 × 0 × 0). - **The east gable with the cut on is the end of a drawn body, not a plate in the void** (second review, N1): the removed half is outlined from the section's plane to the gable along the gable's own profile (`removed-half-outline` in `house/scene.ts`), and the gable itself is a pale plate with its edges at the far end; both follow the east gable (hidden with it, and only while the section is on).
