# Kit requests (what `@onigam/lab-kit` 0.6.0 lacks, from the physics of Bioclimatic House Lab)

Numbered, one paragraph each. The kit was not patched; each item says what the lab does instead. Panel and 3D requests are numbered on, from 7 (the 3D modeler's).

1. **`stackDraft` at altitude.** `sim/thermal`'s `stackDraft(H, Tin, Tbeside)` calls `airRho` at sea-level pressure. At Odeillo, 1,550 m and 84 kPa, the draft comes out 20.6 % too high (tested in `src/sim/figures.test.ts`). This lab writes its own `draftAt(H, Tcolumn, Tbeside, p)` in `src/sim/plant.ts`. Asked: an optional pressure argument, `stackDraft(H, Tin, Tbeside, p = P_ATM)`, the same for `FILM_VERTICAL`: natural convection goes as ρ^(2/3), which this lab applies by hand. Sand, mass heater and solar heater labs are all near sea level and would see no change.
2. **`solar.ts` in the kit.** Half of this lab's physics is the sun, and the kit has none. `src/sim/solar.ts` is a copy of Solar Heater Lab's `src/sim/thermal/solar.ts`, credited, the second lab to carry it. Two additions worth moving with it: `clearSkyPlane` takes the site's altitude (Laue's correction: Meinel's formula alone is 10 to 20 % low at 1,550 m), and a coarse horizon that cuts the beam. `src/sim/optics.ts`, Fresnel on N sheets with a table per glazing, is generic too.
3. **A seeded `rng` outside three.js.** `rng` lives in `@onigam/lab-kit/three`, which the physics must not import. `src/sim/climate.ts` has its own mulberry32, as Solar Heater Lab does. Asked: export it from `@onigam/lab-kit/sim`.
4. **`tubeFlow`'s reversed flow: nothing needed, for the record.** 0.6.0 reads a negative `mdot` as laminar whatever its speed; the kit's next release fixes it with `Math.abs(mdot)` (`lab-kit/docs/sim-thermal.md`). This lab has a reversed flow every clear night, and passes `tubeFlow` the flow's size only, with the sign kept by the loop's own balance (`Plant.readFilms`, `src/sim/earth-tube.ts`), so it gives the same answer on 0.6.0 and after the fix: a test holds it. No bump needed.
5. **A `tubeFriction` that doesn't jump.** Its jump at Re 2300 (documented) is harmless in a forced flow. In a loop driven by a fraction of a pascal the same jump can pin the flow at the transition and print a false plateau, which is precisely the question here (the source's flat-topped curves). `src/sim/plant.ts` uses the larger of 64/Re and Petukhov's instead. Asked: an exported continuous variant, for loops. A question to the kit's owner rather than a need.
6. **Speed labels and a long clock.** Sand Lab's and Solar Heater Lab's requests, still open: the strip writes `×3600`, and every lab on a calendar writes its own `day of year → 21 Jun` (`src/sim/strip.ts` here).
7. **A landscape that is not called `site`.** The kit frames a world called `site` by its authored home pose, gives it camera planes of 0.1 and 4,000 m and a camera that stays above the ground (`Stage.setActive`, `Stage.frameAll`). The research calls this lab's setting `plateau`; to have those three things the 3D names it `site` (label « Le plateau », its parts keep the research's ids), and asks the far plane through `SceneModule.far` (12,000 m: the sky dome and ridges). Asked: a `SceneDef.landscape: true`, or a `SceneModule.landscape`, instead of a name, so that a lab's world ids can be its own. Mass Heater Lab (`homestead`) and Sand Lab hit the same wall and wrote their workarounds in `src/lab.ts`.
8. **A slidable section.** The research asks for a house « cut along a section you can slide »; the kit's `cutaway` is a boolean. The 3D cuts at one fixed plane (through the middle of the sample collector bay, the east half taken away, `dims.ts`: `CUT_X`), which is the section S1's Fig. 1 draws. Asked: `ViewOpts.cutPosition` (0 to 1) and a slider next to the tool, so that a visitor can walk the plane through the room, the wall and the ducts.
9. **What the world is told of the selection, of the camera and of the guide's mode.** `SceneModule.update(state, dt, view)` gets none of them. The `room` world's walls step aside by the side the camera is on (`scene.onBeforeRender`, which sees the camera), and what hangs on a wall goes with it. The earth tube no longer needs more: its three parts are always drawn (pale while the scenario is off, firm when it runs) in a window cut in the ground, with a size and a framing of their own, so they no longer collapse to a point and no longer need a part taken out of the overview's box (`docs/3d.md`). What is still asked: `ViewOpts.camera` (position) and `ViewOpts.selected` (kit request 14 of the other labs asks for the selection), and the guide's mode, since the pale tube stays in the frame of every step that does not name it.
10. **Labels for what is not a part.** The sun's two arcs of the `site` world have a label each (« December sun », « June sun »), and the kit's `Hotspot` needs a `partId`: the labels point at `prototype-house`, which is a lie of one click. Asked: a `Hotspot.partId` that may be null, and then a label that selects nothing.
11. **An instanced part's box.** `PartRegistry.bounds` uses `Box3.setFromObject`, which for an `InstancedMesh` is conservative and reads the geometry's cached bounds: a lab that makes a geometry again (the visitor moves the wall's thickness) must reset `boundingBox` and `boundingSphere` by hand (`src/three/slots.ts`). Asked: the registry to reset them itself, or a note in `docs/3d.md` of the kit.
12. **Helpers the labs copy.** `uvMetric` (UVs in metres on any geometry), `joined`, `HeatStrip` (a one-pixel texture of the model's temperatures on the kit's ramp), the section's `ClipSet` and `Caps` (`src/three/section.ts`), the procedural maps (`textures.ts`) and the instancing of repeated parts (nine collector bays, 63 heliostats, a thousand trees: one draw call each) are the sand lab's, the mass heater lab's and this lab's, copied again. Asked, as in every lab's list: the kit to carry them.
13. **The bottom strip below 1280 px (`docs/qa.md`, D10).** The strip keeps the clock, five speeds, six values and the alarm pills in one row that scrolls sideways: at 768 px the « No alert » pill lies outside the visible strip, at 390 px it is clipped, and with two alarms on, the second ended 17 px outside the strip at 1280 px. This lab shortened its pills to 22 characters at most (`STRIP_ALARMS`, `src/panels/strings.ts`; the panel's status line keeps the long sentences) so that two fit at 1280 px; it cannot do more. Asked: the alarm pill before the values on a narrow strip (or a second row), or a count that opens the list.
14. **A long title and the select's options at 768 and 390 px (`docs/qa.md`, D18).** The top bar cuts the title at about 27 characters on a phone and 38 on a tablet and does not show the tagline; the Simulation's select cuts an option at about 22 characters on a tablet. This lab shortened its title to 20 characters and moved the rest of it to the tagline (`labTitle`, `tagline`, `src/i18n/ui.ts`), and its starting points to 22 characters at most (`PRESET_TEXTS`, `src/sim/texts.ts`). Asked: a title that may take a second line on a narrow bar, or a `brand.shortTitle`, and a select whose open list shows the whole label.
15. **Phone and tablet: labels, the invitation, the scenario's sign (`docs/qa.md`, D19).** « Étiquettes : Toutes » piles the house's labels on the south front at 390 px (nine bays, the recess, the window, the balcony and the rest within a hand's width): asked a density rule by screen size, keeping the main label of each cluster. The speaker's invitation bubble covers two world buttons at 390 px and the first line of the panel's title at 768 px: asked that it dock above the transport bar. After a scenario chip on a phone the sentence that says what the scenario is lies below the chips, outside the sheet's visible part, and the only sign is the select turning to « Autres réglages »: asked that the sheet scroll to it, or a one-line sign in its header. The first Montage step (« Le terrain ») opening on a mostly white view is the 3D's (the step's frame), not the kit's.
16. **Parts outside the opening view (`docs/qa.md`, D22).** The solar furnace and the 1974 building, which the plateau's overview names, are not in its opening view at any width and are found only through their chips. The 3D widens the pose; asked of the kit, for the cases a wider pose cannot solve: a label clamped to the viewport's edge, with an arrow, for a hotspot that lies outside the view, so that a part can be named where it cannot be shown.

## From the material pass (2026-11-13, `docs/3d.md` « Material pass (2026-11-13) »)

17. **An occlusion pass that skips what is not solid (the sister labs' request 25, again).** `SectionAwareGTAOPass` gives every visible mesh a normal material of its own, a transparent one too. The `wall` world's shafts of light (additive sheets in the air, one mesh) stood in that pass as walls and darkened what lay round them in a dash pattern, in the film's look only; the glass and the air's volume are drawn into it the same way. What the lab does: `scene.onBeforeRender` of the `wall` world hides the light's group while the pass has swapped its material (`sunlight.mesh.material !== sunlight.material`). Asked: the pass skips `MeshBasicMaterial`, `ShaderMaterial`, transparent meshes and any mesh with `userData.noOcclusion`, as the sister labs asked.
18. **A sky that is not one colour (the stove lab's request 30, again).** The `wall` world's background is one paper colour and its upper half stays flat through the night; a gradient costs a plane drawn (a draw call and a program, which this budget did not have). The lab fades its ground into the paper with a fog, which hides the horizon's line and not the flat sky. Asked: `SceneModule.backdrop?: { top, horizon }` painted into the clear.
19. **A visible sun for any world.** `src/three/wall/sunlight.ts` draws the sun the state gives (a disc and its glare as a quad that faces the camera at the end of the sun's direction, and shafts of light through the glass: one mesh, one shader, one draw call), and `SUN_UNIFORMS` (`sun.ts`) is the one object the glass reads for its glint. Nothing of it names this lab but the panes of the collector. It belongs next to `makeRig` in the kit, with an option for a world to turn on only the disc.
20. **`display.hide` of the film is defeated by a world that shows its child again.** The film hides the children of a named object and a world's `update` that sets `visible` on one of them (the first version of the sun did, each frame) wins: the hide did nothing and the first look at the shafts read as an artefact of the glass. Asked: the film's hide survives (it hides the object's own meshes by a flag the `update` does not touch), or `FILMS.md` says that a world's own `visible` must be on a parent of what a film may hide.
21. **The material helpers, for every lab (the stove lab's 27 and 31, with what this lab added).** The cut concrete of a section as a data map and a colour map from one field, its shader (`heatMaterial` of `src/three/collector/caps.ts`: the false colour multiplied by the stones, read at three scales, with a stone's chroma pulled to grey, the mean of the multiplier one so that the legend is true), the splat of stones on a tileable map (`stoneLayer` of `textures.ts`: each stone writes its own texels, a quarter of the work of asking every texel for its nine cells), the glass shader (Fresnel to a sky, the sun's glint, `glassLook` of `materials.ts`), and the stove lab's per-triangle `uvMetric` and `uvStretch` (this lab took both; its `flue-pot` wore a map three and a half times too large and nobody saw it until the test did).
