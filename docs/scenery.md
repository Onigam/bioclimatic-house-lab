# Scenery: what stands in the worlds besides the machine

Every prop is built from nothing (boxes, lathes, extrusions, noise-pushed spheres, procedural maps, no file, no canvas, no text), and each one is listed here with what it is for and what drives it. **None of it is a part**, except where a part's own id is named. Nothing says where the houses stand beyond « Odeillo »: the plateau is composed, not mapped (`worlds.test.ts` holds that no coordinate, street or identifier is in any file of `src/three` or in these notes).

## The land (`site` and `house`)

One ground function, `src/three/land/ground.ts`, for both worlds: a granite slope that falls to the south, 2 m across the house's 6.6 m (`dims.ts`: `GROUND_NORTH`, `GROUND_SOUTH`, an estimate within the open data's 2–3.5 m), flat across the yard, rolling away from it, rising to the north. The house stands on it the same way in both worlds: the stepped foundation follows the fall within 5 cm (`machine.test.ts`).

**The ground is not a thing to press on in `site`.** The land, the meadow, the road, the far ridges and the far pines (`pinesFar`) have no raycast: a press on them is a press on nothing, so the kit turns the camera round its target (the selected part) and not round a hill a kilometre off. Their parts are chosen by chip and label; the boulders, the near trees, the buildings and the arcs' labels stay pressable.

**The window in the ground (`house`).** North of the house the land has a hole over the earth tube's plan (cells of the land's own grid), four walls with the strata, a stair of sand beds, and a translucent lid of the same ground (`land.lid`, vertex colours and snow of the land, opacity 0.34, drawn after the pipe): the earth seen in section, with the pipe lying in it. See `house/earthtube.ts`.

## The yard of the house (`site` and `house`)

A washing line with eight garments (swayed by the wind of the parameters), a wheelbarrow with a spade in it, a bucket and a mat at the stair's foot, a vegetable bed, planks under a sheet against the west gable, on the balcony a chair and two pots of geraniums. Each stands on the ground at its own place: `machine.test.ts` measures the lowest point of each against the land.

## The rooms (`room` and `house`)

The living room as the family left it, one function (`room/living.ts`) for both worlds: an oak table with a runner, a teapot, two cups, a bowl of apples, papers and a pen; four ladder-back chairs; an armchair with a knitted blanket thrown over its back (the armchair is the one part: `armchair`); a floor lamp; a woven rug; a rubber plant by the window; pleated curtains tied back; a bookshelf of books in eight colours, a few lying flat; two pictures; a coat on a peg; boots on a mat by the door; a toolbox; a drying rack with the week's washing by the warm wall. The lamps and a point light come on at night (from the sun's altitude in the state). The rest of the house, for the section: two bedrooms (a double bed and a wardrobe; two single beds) and a kitchen with a counter, a table and a chair. The plan of the rooms is an estimate.

**Why shadows and not just stones.** From the film's camera the sun is behind its left shoulder (104° off the axis, a field of 23°): no disc, no glint on the glass, no shadow on the cut (its plane is edge-on to the sun) can be in its frame, and the room floor is in the wall's own shadow (a key-light-only render shows it black). What the frame does see is the ground behind the room, which the sun lights: a shadow on it, long and hard, says where the sun is and that it is high and from the south. The stones are what throws it.

**A grey cover (`site` and `wall`; `house` has no sky).** On a day the state's irradiance is under 40 % of the clear sky's (the recorded 24 December 1974 is a twentieth of the day before's), the `wall`'s sky is an even bright grey, top and horizon within a few levels, with no blue and no warm band; the `site`'s Preetham dome is replaced by a grey that is a little lighter at the horizon, with no sun in it, and its fog goes grey; the key light is a tenth of the sun's (so that no shadow is hard: the stones' shadows and the shafts are gone) and the sky's light rises. The measure is `cover` of `sun.ts` (the complement of `clarity` where both speak), eased as the beam is. **A recorded day is read by its own share of the clear day's radiation** (the table's total over the clear day's at the site, the engine's own `k`: a twentieth on the 24th, 1.06 and 0.99 on the days either side), the whole day long, from its first minute: the dull day's dawn is a grey one and the clear day's is not (round 4 read the state's irradiance against the clear sky's only with the sun 11° or more up and held the value below, so the dawn of the 24th kept the 23rd's blue and flipped to grey in 24 frames at about 09:00). The day rolls at midnight; the cover eases over the night, unseen (the sky's cover is weighted by its day factor: none under −8°), and a clear morning is not a cover whatever the model's ridge does to the beam. A sky that is not recorded has no table to read and keeps round 4's rule (the state's irradiance against the clear sky's, only while the clear sky gives the wall 380 W/m² or more and the sun is 11° or more up, held through dusk, night and dawn). The clouds are still not the model's weather: only the sun it lets through is.

## What the picture does not follow

The wind (a constant 2 m/s in the model) only sways the washing; no snow on the roof, on the trees or on the heliostats; no cloud shadows on the ground; the sky's clouds are Preetham's, not the model's weather (the key light, however, follows the model's cloud: the share of the clear sky's irradiance the state holds, and a dull day is drawn as a grey cover, above); no cloud shadows on the ground; no snow on the `wall`'s ground or its hills.
