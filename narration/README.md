## Register

Pelton Lab's tone (`lab-kit/docs/narration-style.md`): a warm storyteller who says « vous », « on », « nous », short sentences, one image or one wonder per part, a closing detail from the sheet. The thread is **one small ray of sunshine**, « un petit rayon de soleil », a subject and not a guide (« Un seul rayon de soleil suffit à montrer comment », never « suivons »; it does only what the physics gives it, and never chooses, waits or remembers): it crosses two sheets of glass, becomes heat on black paint, chooses the slow road through the concrete (a walk of about four centimetres an hour, fifteen hours through sixty centimetres) and comes out on the room side in the small hours (about three or four in the morning in our figures: « à minuit » was the 45 centimetre wall's result, and went with the fact-check). It is in the introduction and in the parts that meet it (`outer-glazing`, `black-paint`, `storage-wall`, `roof-insulation`). The introduction opens on a December night at Odeillo with a wall that is still warm (« Une nuit de décembre, sur le plateau d'Odeillo », no « Imaginez »), says where the ray ends (« Au petit matin, la pièce garde sa chaleur »), and closes on « Le mur, lui, est encore tiède », a call back and not an order.

## Said as far as the sources go (the independent fact-check of 11 November)

A figure that is our calculation, our drawing or our estimate says so in the sentence that carries it (« notre dessin », « par notre calcul », « notre lecture de la photographie »). A source is credited with what it says, at the strength it says it (« le soleil peut économiser », « un retard d'environ douze heures », « dit l'article »), and a number of ours is never put in the mouth of the inventors: the forty to forty-five centimetres and the twelve hours are theirs, the eleven hours at forty-five centimetres is our closed form. `src/narration.test.ts` holds two tables: `WENT_PAST`, the wordings the fact-check found going past their sources (refused in every segment, with the reason: « à minuit », « n'a jamais existé », « il est habité », « par camion », « plus de soixante-trois », « coulé sur place », « le jet reste chaud », « onze heures de retard », « dix-neuvième siècle », « à midi en hiver, la plaque », « son rôle est pourtant le plus grand », « le seul vrai renouvellement », « les inventeurs annoncent », « conseille un architecte », « ni conduit, ni prise » (the earth tube's last run ends at the fresh-air grille), « des calculs, pas des mesures » (open data and our arithmetic are neither), « nous lisons environ dix-sept » (it is worked out from the paper's figures), and their English), and `KEPT_TO_THE_SOURCE`, the wording that replaces each one, in the segment that says it. The list of what changed, number by number, and of what was not the voice's to change, is in `docs/narration-notes.md`.

**Why the limits are in the parts and not in the introduction.** The owner's rule is that a disclaimer is said once, where it matters. The introduction says once that the model is not tuned; each disagreement is then said in the one segment where the sheets and the lessons say it (the same places as the Simulation's « Là où le modèle ne retrouve pas la source »), with the lessons' own words and figures, never softened. A visitor who plays only the introduction hears that they exist.

**Hedges.** « dans le modèle » is never said (a test holds it). « notre modèle », « notre calcul », « le modèle » appear in 21 segments of 61 in French and 23 in English (a test caps them at 24), where a figure stands against a source or the model is the subject. Estimates are said as estimates by « environ », by « notre estimation » or « notre dessin » where the sheet stars them, never as reported facts.

**No promise, no new law.** No sentence says « safe » or guarantees anything (a test refuses the words). The only lines of law, radon and electricity are the ones the sheets carry: a protected building is not touched without agreement (`solar-houses-1974`), the electric panel is an electrician's job (`electric-panel`), a measurement before any buried pipe where radon is an issue (`earth-tube-drain`). No reference is added and none of the lessons' « from memory » references is spoken.

## Choices the lead may want to change

- The introduction opens on the plateau (not on the house, where the lab opens) for the establishing shot, and ends in the house.
- The `scene:` cues of the introduction hop plateau → wall → room → house: the plateau first so that the visitor sees where the house stands, the wall because it is the heart.
- Titles keep the sheets' names (`Tuyau enterré (et si…)`) and may carry a digit (`La maison de 1967`): a title is shown, never spoken.
- `src/narration.test.ts` reads the private transcripts (no seven-word run of a script may come from them) **only when `NARRATION_TRANSCRIPTS=1`**: this lab's cache also holds what says where the houses stand, so an ordinary `npm test` never opens it. The test prints only the segment and language of a copy, never the words.
