# Narration scripts (French and English)

`fr.json` and `en.json` follow `lab-kit/docs/narration.md`: **61 segments each** (`intro`, 4 worlds, 56 parts: plateau 11, house 18, wall 14, room 13), ids `intro`, `scene-<world>`, `part-<id>`, no lesson segments. The guide is in the **local voice** (`--engine voicebox`, profiles `Onigam narrateur FR` and `EN`, 0 credit, the Voicebox application open): ElevenLabs is not used for this lab. Nobody has listened to it yet; the checks below are what was measured.

Check the length without generating anything (`HOME=<empty dir>` keeps the tool from even opening a key file):

HOME=$(mktemp -d) node ~/Dev/lab-kit/tools/narration/generate.mjs --src narration/en.json --out public/audio/en --dry-run --engine voicebox

## Register

Pelton Lab's tone (`lab-kit/docs/narration-style.md`): a warm storyteller who says « vous », « on », « nous », short sentences, one image or one wonder per part, a closing detail from the sheet. The thread is **one small ray of sunshine**, « un petit rayon de soleil »: it crosses two sheets of glass, becomes heat on black paint, chooses the slow road through the concrete (a walk of about four centimetres an hour, fifteen hours through sixty centimetres) and comes out on the room side in the small hours (about three or four in the morning in our figures: « à minuit » was the 45 centimetre wall's result, and went with the fact-check). It is in the introduction and in the parts that meet it (`outer-glazing`, `black-paint`, `storage-wall`, `roof-insulation`). The introduction opens on a December night at Odeillo with a wall that is still warm, and closes on « le mur est encore tiède » : a call back, not an order.

## Said as far as the sources go (the independent fact-check of 11 November)

A figure that is our calculation, our drawing or our estimate says so in the sentence that carries it (« notre dessin », « par notre calcul », « notre lecture de la photographie »). A source is credited with what it says, at the strength it says it (« le soleil peut économiser », « un retard d'environ douze heures », « dit l'article »), and a number of ours is never put in the mouth of the inventors: the forty to forty-five centimetres and the twelve hours are theirs, the eleven hours at forty-five centimetres is our closed form. `src/narration.test.ts` holds two tables: `WENT_PAST`, the wordings the fact-check found going past their sources (refused in every segment, with the reason: « à minuit », « n'a jamais existé », « il est habité », « par camion », « plus de soixante-trois », « coulé sur place », « le jet reste chaud », « onze heures de retard », « dix-neuvième siècle », « à midi en hiver, la plaque », « son rôle est pourtant le plus grand », « le seul vrai renouvellement », « les inventeurs annoncent », « conseille un architecte », « ni conduit, ni prise » (the earth tube's last run ends at the fresh-air grille), « des calculs, pas des mesures » (open data and our arithmetic are neither), « nous lisons environ dix-sept » (it is worked out from the paper's figures), and their English), and `KEPT_TO_THE_SOURCE`, the wording that replaces each one, in the segment that says it. The list of what changed, number by number, and of what was not the voice's to change, is in `docs/narration-notes.md`.

**Why the limits are in the parts and not in the introduction.** The owner's rule is that a disclaimer is said once, where it matters. The introduction says once that the model is not tuned; each disagreement is then said in the one segment where the sheets and the lessons say it (the same places as the Simulation's « Là où le modèle ne retrouve pas la source »), with the lessons' own words and figures, never softened. A visitor who plays only the introduction hears that they exist.

**Hedges.** « dans le modèle » is never said (a test holds it). « notre modèle », « notre calcul », « le modèle » appear in 21 segments of 61 in French and 23 in English (a test caps them at 24), where a figure stands against a source or the model is the subject. Estimates are said as estimates by « environ », by « notre estimation » or « notre dessin » where the sheet stars them, never as reported facts.

**No promise, no new law.** No sentence says « safe » or guarantees anything (a test refuses the words). The only lines of law, radon and electricity are the ones the sheets carry: a protected building is not touched without agreement (`solar-houses-1974`), the electric panel is an electrician's job (`electric-panel`), a measurement before any buried pipe where radon is an issue (`earth-tube-drain`). No reference is added and none of the lessons' « from memory » references is spoken.

## Cues (150 in each language, the same in both)

`home` 10, `frame` 16, `scene` 3, `select` 1, `mode` 6 (introduction only); `flows` 16, `xray` 16, `cutaway` 6, `isolate` 28, all switched off before the end; `lab:` cues 56 (`day` 6, `hour` 3, `speed` 6, `preset` 10, `flap` 6, `earthtube` 6, `sheets` 4, `vents` 2, `thickness` 1, `scenario` 2, `setpoint` 2). The cue spacing, worlds, tools and simulation state are held by `src/narration.test.ts`:

- At most one camera move (`scene`, `home`, `frame`, `select`) per 35 spoken characters; a part segment has none (it starts framed and selected); only parts of the current world are framed; **never an earth-tube part** (a rule from the days when the tube was drawn only under the what-if and its parts collapsed to a point inside the house; the tube is now always drawn, pale, so framing it would work, but no segment moves the camera and the rule is kept).
- The section only in the house (`cutaway` is used by `floor-slab`, `east-gable`, `west-gable`: a segment that uses it starts without the cut, so the east gable is there to be seen, then the section turns the whole east half into nothing but the gable's pale ghost); X-ray in the house, the wall and the room, never on the plateau; no explode (the lab's worlds come apart in the sheets' tips, not in the voice).
- **The earth tube's three parts** start with `[[lab:earthtube:on]]` and end with `[[lab:earthtube:off]]`, because that is what makes the tube firm up and the figures of the sheet apply (the 3D draws the three parts pale while the what-if is off, since the modeler's pass of 11 November; before it they collapsed to a point inside the house, and the cue was what made them appear). `intake` says « La voilà plus ferme » (« There it firms up »), not « qui apparaît » (« There it appears »).
- **The simulation is left as it was found.** Every segment that touches it ends either with `[[lab:preset:prototype-1967]]` or with a hand restore (`flap:winter`, `vents:open`, `setpoint:20`, `sheets:2`, `earthtube:off`, `speed:3600`). The QA review (D14) found the starting-point select reading « Autres réglages » after eight such segments, because the engine turns the label to `custom` on any change; `runCue` (`src/cues.ts`, `startingPointOf`) now gives the label back when every value is a starting point's again (not while a scenario runs, and not while a visitor's own change is still there), and `src/narration.test.ts` runs every segment's `[[lab:…]]` cues on a fresh simulation and holds the label at the end. The clock opens at ×3600; a day a second (`speed:86400`) is used only to show the wave in the wall (`scene-wall`, `storage-wall`, `chart-recorder`), and a scenario is never run at that speed: its timed events run on the real-seconds clock (24 s between two events), so at ×86400 they would pass in a blink.
- **Timing against the model, measured headless** (`HouseSim`, the lab's own engine): in `the-night`, the flow runs 24.5 L/s at 17 h, falls to zero about ten seconds after the start and is reversed (−10 to −12 L/s) from about twelve to fifteen seconds. `summer-flap` places the scenario cue about eight seconds before the sentence that says « regardez les points d'air ». `christmas-1974` starts on 22 December: the 24th is three days of audio away, so `inner-wall-face` does not claim to show it, it only starts the recorded days.
- The introduction goes through the plateau, the wall, the room and the house (it starts on the plateau, ends in the house, where the lab opens), then the six modes in the order of the rail, and clears the selection.

## What the sheets say and the voice does not

- The sheets' legend of marks (†, \*), the formulas, the specifications tables, the checks and the care paragraphs stay on the sheets; the voice keeps one closing detail from them (the lid, the rug in front of the grille, the dirty dome, the rust under the bars).
- The lessons' tables stay in the lessons: the satellite service's kilowatt-hours per square metre, the inventors' twenty days, the test rooms' kelvins, the German passive house's figures. The voice keeps the one image each time (a zoologist's black plate, the sun of the day before).
- Said in the voice as the sheets say them, but worth a second look by the lead (the sheets read them only in summary, `docs/data.md`): the protection of the 1974 building (« un bâtiment protégé », `solar-houses-1974`), the radon line (`earth-tube-drain`, no commune, no zone), the patent (`black-paint`, « en mille huit cent quatre-vingt-un », a zoologist, no name).
- Not spoken, by choice: the sheets' guide warnings (weight, glass at height, mains, vents shut in summer, fresh air and carbon monoxide) belong to the assembly and installation guides, in the steps where the gesture is risky; the introduction does not repeat them and no part gives a safety promise.

## Choices the lead may want to change

- The introduction opens on the plateau (not on the house, where the lab opens) for the establishing shot, and ends in the house.
- The `scene:` cues of the introduction hop plateau → wall → room → house: the plateau first so that the visitor sees where the house stands, the wall because it is the heart.
- Titles keep the sheets' names (`Tuyau enterré (et si…)`) and may carry a digit (`La maison de 1967`): a title is shown, never spoken.
- `src/narration.test.ts` reads the private transcripts (no seven-word run of a script may come from them) **only when `NARRATION_TRANSCRIPTS=1`**: this lab's cache also holds what says where the houses stand, so an ordinary `npm test` never opens it. The test prints only the segment and language of a copy, never the words.
